## Judge criteria (top two first)

- 1) Coin a new English word and define it in a single clear line (not an existing word, not a non-definition).
- 2) The word fills a real gap: it names something English has no word for and a reader recognises at once; the definition is precise and self-sufficient in one reading.
- Also: sayable, spellable, memorable word form; fresh, unexpected pairing.

## Candidates (all ASCII, no trailing newline in the entry)

### 1. 100 chars — PICK

> Tabnap (n.): the micro-nap you take with fifteen browser tabs open, convinced you are still working.

PICK. The word is a clean, pronounceable blend (tab + nap) that names a moment everyone recognises but English has no word for — a real gap. The definition lands in one reading, needs no second sentence, and the twist ('convinced you are still working') makes it memorable.

### 2. 99 chars

> Unsendxiety (n.): the panic after hitting send, spent rereading a message you can no longer unsend.

Strong gap (post-send regret) and a sayable blend, but the word is slightly harder to spell/say at first sight than 'Tabnap', and the feeling overlaps with plain 'regret'.

### 3. 121 chars

> Notifright (n.): the jolt when a notification preview reveals just enough to worry you, but not enough to explain itself.

Real gap and self-sufficient definition, but the word reads a little forced ('notif-' is an awkward clipping) and the definition is the longest of the three.
